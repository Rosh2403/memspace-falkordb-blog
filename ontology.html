The Memspace ontology

Schema 1.2.0: 13 types, 49 relationships, and the rules that keep your assistants' facts honest. This is the reference behind the story.

AGPJRP Team Santology Reference · October 18, 2026

Memspace doesn't keep your chats. It keeps facts: typed things, typed relationships between them, and the exact words each fact came from.

The schema is defined in one place in the code (src/ontology.ts), validated with Zod, and served live to assistants through the get_ontology tool. The dashboard's Schema panel reads the same registry. It's an evidence-backed property graph. There's no OWL reasoner and no RDF export, and nothing is inferred behind your back.

01The rules

These ship with the schema and every assistant sees them. They're short on purpose.

  1. Private by default; membership is not sharing consent.
  2. Stable keys are scoped to the author; names do not merge people or projects.
  3. In a shared space, a person mentioned in a snapshot is shown as the space member it unambiguously names (the author, or the only member with that full or first name). Private graphs are never merged.
  4. Entity properties describe identity. Changing states, deadlines and preferences are claims.
  5. A shared claim is a snapshot; source revisions do not silently republish it.
  6. Evidence is an attributed excerpt, not independent verification.
  7. Forgetting a source removes its claims and publications. Retraction stops future reads.
  8. An explicit negation is a claim with negated=true; it is never stored as the affirmative fact.
  9. When no relationship fits, extraction may propose a provisional one. It is usable immediately, reused by later extractions, and approved, merged or rejected by an ontology reviewer.

02From a note to facts

Extraction runs on the server, so assistants don't need to call anything extra. A save looks like this:

  1. 1

    Save

    remember or revise_memory writes the note and its revision. A save never waits on the model.

  2. 2

    Queue

    The same write creates an OntologyJob for that exact revision. The save handler starts it right away.

  3. 3

    Propose

    The model gets the note, the live schema, and up to 300 of the author's own current facts as candidates. No tools, no write access, nobody else's graph.

  4. 4

    Check

    Types, endpoints and allowed values must match the schema, every quote must appear in the note, and the note must still be on that revision.

  5. 5

    Write

    Valid facts are stored against the revision they came from, at most 24 entities and 20 facts per note. Invalid ones are counted and dropped.

  6. 6

    Index and share

    Facts get full-text and vector entries. If the note is already shared into a space, its facts are published there too.

A worker checks for unfinished jobs every five seconds. Each job is claimed with a 120-second lease, so two workers never run the same one, and retried with backoff up to five attempts. The lease and the note's revision are checked again after the model answers, so a note that was edited or forgotten mid-extraction never receives stale facts.

Every save returns a status your assistant can read back to you:

StatusWhat it means
structuredFacts were written. The receipt lists their IDs.
no_supported_claimsNothing in the note fit the schema. The note is kept and shows "Not in ontology".
pendingStill running, or waiting to retry.
failedFive attempts didn't succeed. The owner can choose Retry ontology on the dashboard.
supersededThe note changed while extraction ran. The newer revision gets its own job.
not_configuredNo extraction model is set up on this deployment.

What never becomes a fact: hypotheticals, questions, role-play and quoted instructions. Learning something isn't having the skill, an assignment isn't acceptance, a visit isn't residence, and a wish to change jobs isn't new employment. Relative dates stay vague instead of being turned into made-up timestamps.

05Identity and time

Every entity has a key scoped to its author. Two people using the same name, or even the same key, don't merge. When you say "I" or "me", the model uses a stable self key for you, the author, never for the assistant you're talking to.

Reusing an existing entity needs more than a matching name. It has to be the same note's earlier revision, or the new note has to quote context that singles out exactly one existing candidate. Events, tasks and debts are only reused within the same note's history, so two trips or two loans stay separate.

Facts carry three kinds of time. recordedAt is when it was captured. Optional validFrom and validUntil say when it applies, with validUntil exclusive. Deadlines and event times need an explicit time zone; if the note didn't give one, the fact isn't written with a guessed one.

A correction names the fact it supersedes, and only the same author can supersede their own fact. If someone else reports something different, both stay, each attributed to whoever said it.

06Sharing

There are two ways facts reach a space.

  • Share one fact. publish_claim copies a single fact into a space as a frozen snapshot: the assertion, its quote, who said it, and the entities' types and names. Private aliases, the source note's ID and the correction history stay out. Later edits to your note don't silently change what teammates already received.
  • Share a whole note. share_memory shares the note and publishes its current facts alongside it. When the note is revised or re-extracted those snapshots are replaced, and when you take the note back they're withdrawn. A fact you'd already shared on its own is left alone.

Inside a space, a person mentioned in a shared fact resolves to the member it names: the author themselves, or the only member whose full or first name matches. That member becomes one node, with what the author actually wrote kept as an alias. If the name is ambiguous it links to nobody, and everything is re-linked whenever someone joins or is removed.

Reads always need current membership. Retracting stops future reads, and forgetting a note scrubs its facts and anything published from them. Content another assistant already showed someone can't be pulled back.

07Growing the schema

When no relationship fits something a person actually said, extraction may propose new ones, up to three per note. Each proposal is normalized to an UPPER_SNAKE name between 3 and 40 characters, and anything that reads like an instruction is rejected.

Near-duplicates collapse. A proposal reuses an existing relationship with the same root, so CAN_SPEAK becomes SPEAKS, or a broader or narrower one whose types already fit. A provisional relationship is only saved once a valid fact actually uses it. Each author can add five a day, and at most 100 can wait for review. Later extractions see the provisional list, but as data, not instructions.

Reviewers are set per deployment with ONTOLOGY_ADMIN_EMAILS. From the dashboard's Schema panel they can:

  • Approve it, making it a normal relationship.
  • Merge it into another relationship whose types fit every existing fact. The facts and their space snapshots are rewritten.
  • Reject it. Its facts are retired, their snapshots withdrawn and their search entries deleted, and the same root can't be proposed again.

Review shows names, types and counts only. A reviewer never sees anyone's facts or quotes.

08Limits we know about

  • A quote shows where a fact came from. It doesn't prove the model understood the sentence, and nothing here verifies real-world truth.
  • Matching against existing entities looks at the author's 300 most recent facts and 40 candidates, so older entities can be missed and created again.
  • Names alone never merge. The same project in four notes can appear as four nodes until a later note gives enough context to connect them.
  • Older notes aren't reinterpreted when the schema changes. The owner can retry extraction on notes marked "Not in ontology".
  • Recall expands one hop. There's no multi-hop reasoning, formal inference, or automatic conflict resolution between authors.
